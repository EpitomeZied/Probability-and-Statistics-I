# S03 — Probability axioms

> [!NOTE]
> Probability and Statistics I, section 03. Index: [Probability and Statistics I Sections](../section-plan.md) · questions: [Probability and Statistics I Question Bank](../../practice/question-bank.md) · teaching notes: [Probability and Statistics I Playbook](../teaching-playbook.md).

**One idea to land:** three axioms, and everything else in the first half of the course is derived from them.

**Board:**
1. Sample space drawn for a real experiment; events as subsets. Then the axioms: non-negativity, total mass 1, additivity for disjoint events.
2. Addition rule with the Venn overlap visible: `P(A∪B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A∩B)`. Derive it, do not state it.
3. Complement rule, and the "at least one" pattern that leans on it.
4. **Mutually exclusive ≠ independent.** Put both definitions on the board together and force the contrast — this confusion survives all term if unaddressed.

**They solve:** dice and card problems with unions and complements; then "at least one six in four rolls" via the complement.

**Mistakes I expect:** adding probabilities of overlapping events; conflating disjoint and independent; probabilities summing past 1 without noticing; forgetting that the complement needs the *same* sample space.
