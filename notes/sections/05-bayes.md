# S05 — Bayes

> [!NOTE]
> Probability and Statistics I, section 05. Index: [Probability and Statistics I Sections](../section-plan.md) · questions: [Probability and Statistics I Question Bank](../../practice/question-bank.md) · teaching notes: [Probability and Statistics I Playbook](../teaching-playbook.md).

**One idea to land:** the **base rate dominates**, and the reason their intuition fails is that it ignores the base rate entirely.

**Board:**
1. Run the medical-test problem with *counts out of 100,000*, not probabilities — on a two-by-two table. Nearly everyone gets it right this way and nearly everyone gets it wrong in symbols. Show both, in that order.
2. Then the formula, mapped onto the table cells they just filled in, so it is a summary of something they already believe.
3. Law of total probability as the denominator, drawn as "all the ways to get here."
4. Prior → evidence → posterior, named as a pipeline.

**They solve:** two base-rate problems with different priors (same test, rare vs common disease) so they *see* the prior move the answer.

**Mistakes I expect:** ignoring the prior; swapping the conditional; forgetting to sum all paths in the denominator; treating a 99%-accurate test as giving a 99% posterior.

**Close:** "When the answer feels wrong, build the table of counts. The table has never lied to me."
