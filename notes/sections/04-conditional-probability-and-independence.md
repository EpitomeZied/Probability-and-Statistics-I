# S04 — Conditional probability and independence

> [!NOTE]
> Probability and Statistics I, section 04. Index: [Probability and Statistics I Sections](../section-plan.md) · questions: [Probability and Statistics I Question Bank](../../practice/question-bank.md) · teaching notes: [Probability and Statistics I Playbook](../teaching-playbook.md).

**One idea to land:** conditioning **shrinks the sample space**. `P(A|B)` is a probability in the world where B happened.

**Board:**
1. The definition with the Venn picture, and the denominator drawn as the new universe.
2. `P(A|B)` vs `P(B|A)` with a story where they differ enormously — "probability of a positive test given disease" vs "disease given positive." This is the hook for S05.
3. Tree diagram for a two-stage experiment; multiply along branches, add across branches.
4. Independence as a *testable* claim: `P(A∩B) = P(A)P(B)`. Check it on a real table rather than asserting it.
5. With vs without replacement, drawn as two different trees.

**They solve:** two-stage draws with and without replacement; then verify independence from a contingency table.

**Mistakes I expect:** inverting the conditional; assuming independence because the events "feel unrelated"; using the with-replacement tree for a without-replacement story; multiplying across branches instead of along them.
