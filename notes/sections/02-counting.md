# S02 — Counting

> [!NOTE]
> Probability and Statistics I, section 02. Index: [Probability and Statistics I Sections](../section-plan.md) · questions: [Probability and Statistics I Question Bank](../../practice/question-bank.md) · teaching notes: [Probability and Statistics I Playbook](../teaching-playbook.md).

**One idea to land:** **choose the model before computing** — ordered or not, repetition allowed or not. The arithmetic never was the hard part.

**Board:**
1. The decision tree, drawn once and reused all term: order matters + no repetition → permutation; order irrelevant → combination; repetition allowed → powers or stars-and-bars.
2. Sum vs product rule, and the classic double-count.
3. Equally-likely outcomes: `P = favourable / total` — and the loud warning that this only holds when outcomes really are equally likely.
4. Verify every count on a tiny case by listing it. This habit saves them in the exam.

**They solve:** committees with "at least one" (forcing the complement), password counts with constraints, dealing hands from a deck.

**Mistakes I expect:** permutation where combination was meant; counting "at least one" directly; assuming equal likelihood when the setup does not give it; double counting overlapping cases.
