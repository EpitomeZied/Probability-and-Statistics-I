# S06 — Discrete random variables

> [!NOTE]
> Probability and Statistics I, section 06. Index: [Probability and Statistics I Sections](../section-plan.md) · questions: [Probability and Statistics I Question Bank](../../practice/question-bank.md) · teaching notes: [Probability and Statistics I Playbook](../teaching-playbook.md).

**One idea to land:** a random variable is a **function from outcomes to numbers**. The PMF is the bookkeeping.

**Board:**
1. Map a sample space to values explicitly (two dice → sum) and draw the PMF as a stick plot.
2. The two requirements of a PMF: non-negative, sums to 1 — always verified, never assumed.
3. CDF built from the PMF, drawn as the staircase; then `P(a < X ≤ b)` read off it, with attention to which endpoint is included.

**They solve:** build the PMF for the sum of two dice; compute three probabilities from it; sketch the CDF.

**Mistakes I expect:** PMF that does not sum to 1 (and no check); confusing the value of X with its probability; endpoint errors with `<` vs `≤`; treating the CDF as if it were the PMF.
