# S11 — Sampling distributions and the CLT

> [!NOTE]
> Probability and Statistics I, section 11. Index: [Probability and Statistics I Sections](../section-plan.md) · questions: [Probability and Statistics I Question Bank](../../practice/question-bank.md) · teaching notes: [Probability and Statistics I Playbook](../teaching-playbook.md).

**One idea to land:** **x̄ is itself a random variable**, with its own distribution, and that distribution is narrower than the population's.

**Board:**
1. The conceptual leap, drawn: one population → many samples → one x̄ each → the histogram of those x̄'s. Draw all three panels. This is the hardest idea in the course and it is entirely a picture.
2. `E[x̄] = μ` and `SE = σ/√n`; then the √n asked about explicitly — four times the data for half the error.
3. CLT stated with its conditions, then demonstrated: means of samples from a *skewed* population going bell-shaped as n grows. Simulate it live if possible; it converts disbelief instantly.
4. Standard deviation vs standard error, written side by side, because the distinction is silently assumed by every later formula.

**They solve:** probability that a sample mean exceeds a value; then the same with n quadrupled, and a sentence on what changed.

**Mistakes I expect:** using σ where σ/√n belongs (the single most common error of the second half); applying the CLT to one observation; assuming the *population* becomes normal; forgetting the finite-population or independence caveats.
