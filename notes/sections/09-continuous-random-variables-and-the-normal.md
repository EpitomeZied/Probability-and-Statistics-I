# S09 — Continuous random variables and the normal

> [!NOTE]
> Probability and Statistics I, section 09. Index: [Probability and Statistics I Sections](../section-plan.md) · questions: [Probability and Statistics I Question Bank](../../practice/question-bank.md) · teaching notes: [Probability and Statistics I Playbook](../teaching-playbook.md).

**One idea to land:** for a continuous variable, **probability is area**, and the probability of any exact value is zero.

**Board:**
1. Density vs PMF: why `f(x)` can exceed 1, and why `P(X = 3) = 0`. Shade an interval to get a probability.
2. Total area = 1 as the continuous version of "sums to 1"; a uniform density to make it concrete.
3. The normal curve: symmetry, the 68–95–99.7 landmarks, and standardisation `z = (x − μ)/σ` drawn as relabelling the axis.
4. Table use in **both** directions: `x → probability`, and `probability → x` (the percentile direction, which the exam asks and they never practise).
5. `P(X > a) = 1 − P(X ≤ a)`, always with the shaded picture.

**They solve:** four normal problems, including one "find the cutoff for the top 10%"; every answer must come with a sketch.

**Mistakes I expect:** forgetting to standardise; sign errors in z; looking up the wrong tail; using σ² where σ belongs; treating a density value as a probability.
