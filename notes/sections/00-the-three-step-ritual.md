# S00 — The three-step ritual

> [!NOTE]
> Probability and Statistics I, section 00. Index: [Probability and Statistics I Sections](../section-plan.md) · questions: [Probability and Statistics I Question Bank](../../practice/question-bank.md) · teaching notes: [Probability and Statistics I Playbook](../teaching-playbook.md).

**One idea to land:** the formula is the last thing you write, not the first.

**Board:**
1. One story problem. I refuse to compute anything until the three steps are on the board. Do this theatrically; it sets the tone.
2. Two problems with the *same numbers* and different models (sampling with vs without replacement) to prove the numbers are not the question.
3. Notation, pinned for the term: `P(A∩B)`, `P(A|B)`, `P(A∪B)`, and what each sounds like in words.

**Their turn:** three short stories, and for each they write only the three steps — no computation. Then we compare across the room and watch identical stories produce different variable definitions.

**Mistakes I expect:** grabbing the first formula that has matching letters; defining X vaguely ("X = the sample"); never stating an assumption.

**Close:** "Give me the random variable in words, with units, and half the marks are already yours."
